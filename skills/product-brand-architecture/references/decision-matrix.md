# Product Brand Architecture Decision Matrix

## Score options

Compare each candidate architecture from 1 to 5 on:

| Dimension | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Product clarity | Can a new user explain what each product does? |
| Trust separation | Does the verifier remain independent from the actor? |
| Audience coherence | Do the brands share enough audience to benefit from a parent? |
| Discovery | Can users find a product from the problem they already understand? |
| Operational load | Can the team maintain every site and account? |
| Extensibility | Can new products join without renaming the portfolio? |
| Collision risk | Are names, domains, companies, and product categories clear? |
| Migration cost | Can existing audience, SEO, and links move safely? |

State weights before scoring. Do not hide a critical legal, trust, or collision problem inside an average.

## Common topologies

### Branded house

One company brand contains all products. Use when audiences and promises strongly overlap. Risk: one product failure affects all and trust roles may blur.

### House of products

Each product leads with its own brand. Use when audiences, categories, or acquisition paths differ. Risk: duplicated operations and weak parent recognition.

### Endorsed portfolio

A neutral parent supports distinct product brands. Use when products are complementary but need separate promises or trust boundaries. This is often the strongest fit for an execution product plus an independent verifier.

## Domain checklist

Before recommending purchase:

- search current web and product use;
- check RDAP or registrar status;
- check likely spelling and pronunciation confusion;
- inspect Companies House or the relevant company registry;
- flag trademark clearance as a separate legal check;
- compare `.com`, product-relevant alternatives, and redirect roles;
- state that checkout is final authority;
- stop after the smallest coherent portfolio. Do not encourage speculative hoarding.

## Separate-account gate

Return `create now`, `defer`, or `do not create` for each proposed account. Include audience, product maturity, content supply, operator capacity, and the date or evidence that will trigger reassessment.
