# Platform Native Campaign

Adapt one truth into several native expressions. Preserve the claim and evidence while changing the presentation.

## Required inputs

- audience and desired user outcome;
- Evidence Story Bank record or equivalent source;
- approved claims and prohibited claims;
- verified destination and CTA;
- available proof assets;
- brand voice and account identity;
- platform rules and current limits.

If evidence is missing, return a research or capture task instead of filling the gap with generic copy.

## Adaptation rules

- Threads: concise observation, disagreement, or question. Optimise for conversation and language learning.
- Instagram: visual explanation, carousel, reel, or annotated proof. The caption supports the visual rather than duplicating every frame.
- Reddit: answer the community's problem first, respect subreddit rules, disclose product affiliation when relevant, and avoid link repetition.
- LinkedIn: connect technical evidence to engineering leadership, risk, delivery, or business implications.
- Facebook: fit the sophistication and norms of the specific group or audience. Avoid generic page spam.
- GitHub: make claims reproducible through current READMEs, examples, releases, issues, and exact evidence links.
- Google Business Profile: lead with a local customer outcome, use a purpose-built image, validated CTA, and tracked destination.

Do not copy and paste identical text across networks. Do not invent metrics, testimonials, or product capability. Do not turn every community reply into promotion.

## Campaign packet

Return:

1. shared story and claim map;
2. platform role and hypothesis;
3. platform-native copy or storyboard;
4. asset specification and source evidence;
5. CTA and tracked destination;
6. approval fingerprint inputs;
7. success metric and learning question;
8. recommended publication sequence.

Read [references/channels.md](references/channels.md) for detailed content shapes, launch sequencing, and channel-specific failure modes.
